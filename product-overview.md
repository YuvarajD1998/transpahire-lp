# Transpahire – Product Overview

---

## 1. Product Summary

Transpahire is an intelligent hiring platform built for modern talent teams.

It helps organizations find the right candidates faster by combining structured skill matching with AI-powered recommendations. At the same time, it gives candidates a smart, transparent experience — showing them exactly why they are a good fit for a role and helping them present themselves in the best light.

Transpahire sits between a traditional applicant tracking system (ATS) and a smart talent marketplace. It is lighter and faster than enterprise ATS tools, but far more intelligent than a basic job board.

**Core value proposition:**

- Organizations post jobs and discover pre-scored, best-fit candidates automatically
- Candidates build rich profiles and receive personalized job recommendations with clear match explanations
- Recruiters spend less time searching and more time having the right conversations
- Hiring decisions are backed by data, not gut feel

## 3. Core Modules

---

### Job Management

**Overview:**
This is where hiring starts. Organizations create job postings, define what skills and experience they need, and control the entire lifecycle of a position — from first draft to filled.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Create job postings with titles, descriptions, locations, salary ranges, and work style (remote, hybrid, on-site)
- Upload a job description document and have the skills and requirements extracted automatically
- Define required skills and mark each one by how important it is — critical, required, preferred, or a bonus
- Set expected experience levels and seniority for the role
- Move jobs through a structured workflow: Draft → Under Review → Approved → Published → Active → Closed
- Pause a job temporarily without losing its setup
- Duplicate an existing job to quickly create a similar role
- Control whether salary is visible to candidates
- Assign team members to own and work a specific job

**User Value:**
Hiring teams can set up a well-defined role in minutes and control every aspect of how it reaches candidates. The approval workflow ensures quality before a job goes live. Skills attached to a job become the basis for intelligent matching.

---

### Candidate Profiles

**Overview:**
This module powers how candidates present themselves on the platform. A rich, complete profile unlocks better job matches and better visibility to recruiters.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Personal information: name, location, headline, professional summary, contact links
- Skills section with proficiency level (beginner to expert) and years of experience per skill
- Work experience timeline with descriptions and highlights
- Education history with institution and degree details
- Projects and portfolio items
- Certifications
- Social profiles (LinkedIn, GitHub, personal website)
- Privacy control — choose who can see the profile (public, limited, or private)
- Profile completeness tracker that shows exactly what is still missing

**User Value:**
The more complete a candidate's profile, the more accurately the platform can match them to the right jobs. A strong profile also increases the chances of being surfaced in recruiter searches.

---

### Resume Intelligence

**Overview:**
Candidates upload their existing resumes and the platform does the heavy lifting — extracting skills, experience, and education automatically.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Upload a resume in any common format
- AI-powered extraction of skills, work history, education, certifications, and projects
- Confidence indicators on each extracted item (so candidates know what was confidently detected vs. uncertain)
- Review screen: candidates can accept, reject, or edit extracted data before it's added to their profile
- Support for multiple resumes (set one as the primary)
- AI resume critique: scores the resume on quality dimensions and provides specific suggestions for improvement — such as adding measurable outcomes or reducing generic language
- Resume quality indicators shown to recruiters as a trust signal

**User Value:**
Candidates with a well-written resume can populate their profile in seconds rather than typing everything by hand. The critique feature helps them improve how they present themselves. Recruiters get a quality signal that helps them prioritize candidates with substantive, authentic profiles.

---

### Smart Matching Engine

**Overview:**
The core intelligence of the platform. When a recruiter looks for candidates for a job, or a candidate looks for jobs, the matching engine ranks results by how well each person or role fits — and explains why.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Every candidate-to-job pairing gets a match score from 0–100
- Scores are broken down into readable dimensions: skill coverage, experience alignment, location preference, salary alignment, and AI semantic similarity
- Match classifications: Strong Match, Good Match, Potential, Weak
- Explanation view: see exactly which required skills are covered, which are partial, and which are missing
- AI-generated narrative: a 2–3 sentence plain-language explanation of why a candidate is or isn't a good fit
- Hidden talent detection: surfaces candidates who don't have a skill listed but have closely related experience that transfers
- Career trajectory analysis: detects growth patterns (consistent growth, career pivot, specialist, generalist)
- Seniority alignment: flags candidates who are over- or under-leveled for the role
- Potential score: rates how likely a candidate is to grow into the role even if not a perfect fit today
- Responsiveness signal: estimates how likely a passive candidate is to engage

**User Value:**
Recruiters get ranked results they can trust, with clear reasons they can act on. They stop wading through profiles that look good on the surface but are actually poor fits. Candidates understand exactly where they stand — no more guessing after applying.

---

### Recruiter Controls and Tuning

**Overview:**
Recruiters can customize how matching works for each job — adjusting the weight of specific skills and simulating different scenarios.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Adjust how much a specific skill matters for a job (e.g., make Python more important than SQL)
- Re-rank the matched candidate list instantly after adjustments
- What-if simulation: see how adding, removing, or changing a skill requirement affects the candidate pool
- JD optimization report: flags common issues in job requirements — too many critical skills, skills that almost no candidates have, or missing soft-skill tiers
- Fairness monitoring: checks if the matching results show patterns that might unfairly favor or exclude certain experience bands

**User Value:**
No two jobs are exactly alike. Recruiters can fine-tune matching for their specific context without needing a data scientist. The fairness report builds confidence that hiring decisions are based on merit.

---

### Candidate Search

**Overview:**
A standalone search experience for recruiters and sourcers to proactively discover candidates beyond matched rankings.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Structured search: filter candidates by skills, years of experience, location, education level, certifications, availability (notice period, open-to-work status), expected salary, and profile quality
- Semantic search: type a description in plain text ("backend engineer with 5 years of fintech experience and Python") and get relevant results
- Quick-view candidate drawer: see a full candidate profile without leaving the search page
- Paginated, fast-loading results with infinite scroll
- Skill chips and match highlights shown on each candidate card

**User Value:**
Sourcers can rapidly build shortlists whether they know exactly what they want (structured) or are exploring an idea (semantic). The two modes work together to eliminate dead-end searches.

---

### Application Tracking

**Overview:**
Both candidates and recruiters can track where every application stands in the hiring process.

**Key Capabilities:**

For candidates:
- See all submitted applications in one place
- View the current status of each application (submitted, viewed, shortlisted, interview scheduled, offered, rejected)
- View interview details and recruiter feedback when shared

For recruiters and hiring managers:
- See all applicants for a job in a single list
- Filter by application status
- Update status at each stage
- Add feedback and ratings (public or private) to applications
- Schedule interviews with candidate details
- View interview history per candidate

**User Value:**
No more losing track of who is where. Both sides of the hiring process have a clear view of what is happening, which reduces missed follow-ups and keeps candidates engaged.

---

### Hiring Pipeline

**Overview:**
Each job has its own custom pipeline — a visual board where the hiring team can move candidates from one stage to the next.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Default pipeline stages: Sourced, Reviewed, Shortlisted, Interviewing, Offer, Hired/Rejected
- Create custom stages with names and colors per job
- Reorder stages to match the team's actual process
- Track how candidates flow through stages with timestamps and notes
- See how many candidates are at each stage at a glance
- Add candidates from search directly into a job's pipeline
- Track the original source of each candidate (referral, LinkedIn, job board, direct application, etc.)

**User Value:**
Hiring moves faster when the team has a shared, up-to-date view of where every candidate stands. Custom pipelines match how teams actually work rather than forcing a rigid process.

---

### Hiring Analytics

**Overview:**
Data and reporting for teams that want to understand how their hiring is performing.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Funnel view for each job: how many candidates reach each stage
- Conversion rates from application to hire
- Skill gap analysis: which required skills are hardest to find in the candidate pool
- Pipeline health: which stages have bottlenecks (candidates stagnating longer than expected)
- Time-to-hire tracking by stage
- Organization-wide hiring trends: match-to-hire ratios, average scores at hire, prediction accuracy
- Pool intelligence: how many qualified candidates exist in the system for a given job
- Skill heatmap: which skills are most and least common in the talent pool

**User Value:**
Hiring managers can see where the process is breaking down and fix it. JD managers can understand if their job requirements are realistic given available talent. Leaders can track hiring velocity and quality over time.

---

### Talent Pool Intelligence

**Overview:**
Market-level insights about the talent available for a given job or skill set.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Pool size estimate for a given role
- Score distribution of available candidates
- Per-skill coverage — what percentage of the pool has each required skill
- Scarcity index for critical skills: High, Medium, Low
- Skill demand trends (emerging vs. declining skills)
- Candidate-to-candidate similarity: find candidates who are similar to a candidate you already like

**User Value:**
Hiring teams can set realistic expectations early. If a critical skill is scarce, they know to either adjust requirements or extend the search timeline before it becomes a problem.

---

### Skill and Job Taxonomy

**Overview:**
The intelligent backbone of the platform. A structured, comprehensive catalog of skills and job roles that powers matching, search, and recommendations across the entire product.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Hierarchical skill catalog: from broad categories down to specific tools and technologies
- Skill types: technical, soft skills, domain expertise, tools, certifications, languages
- Skill relationships: one skill can require another, enable another, or be similar to another
- Skill clusters: curated groupings of related skills (e.g., a "frontend stack" cluster)
- Hierarchical job role catalog: from broad job families to specific titles
- Seniority levels per role (intern to distinguished/executive)
- Career progression maps: what roles typically lead to which other roles
- International standards alignment: ESCO (European), O*NET (US occupational) framework mapping
- Market intelligence: demand scores and trending indicators per skill

For platform admins:
- Full catalog management with quality review workflows
- Duplicate detection and merging
- Bulk import and update tools

**User Value:**
Because skills are structured and interconnected, the platform understands nuance that keyword search misses. A candidate with "React" experience is recognized as having relevant knowledge for a "Next.js" job. A "Software Engineer II" is understood as different from a "Staff Engineer." This structure is what makes matching accurate rather than approximate.

**Overview:**
Tools for administering teams, roles, and accounts across the platform.

**Key Capabilities:**
- Create and manage team member accounts within an organization
- Assign org roles: Admin, Job Manager, Hiring Manager, Recruiter, Sourcer, Interviewer
- Activate, suspend, or remove user accounts
- Manager-recruiter hierarchy (managers can oversee and monitor their team's activity)
- Full audit trail: every action taken is logged with who did it and when
- Email verification for all new accounts
- Secure password reset flows

**User Value:**
Organizations can control exactly who can see and do what. Sensitive candidate data is only visible to the right people. Changes are always traceable.

---

### Location Intelligence

**Overview:**
A smart location system that powers consistent matching and search across cities and regions.

**Key Capabilities:**
- City autocomplete across 148,000+ cities worldwide
- Major cities surface first in results
- Country filtering for region-relevant suggestions
- Alias resolution: searching "Bangalore" and "Bengaluru" refer to the same city — candidates and jobs always match correctly regardless of spelling variation
- Same behavior for other common aliases worldwide (Bombay/Mumbai, Calcutta/Kolkata, etc.)

**User Value:**
Location mismatches due to alternate city names never cause a candidate to be missed. The location experience feels natural and instant, like a modern travel or food delivery app.

## 5. Core Workflows

### Workflow 1 — Recruiter finds and hires a candidate

1. **A job is created.** A job manager defines the role title, description, required skills, seniority, location, and salary.
2. **Skills are assigned importance.** The team marks which skills are critical (must have), required, preferred, or a nice-to-have bonus.
3. **The job goes through review.** Before it's published, it moves through an internal approval process.
4. **The job goes live.** Candidates can now find and apply.
5. **The platform surfaces matches.** The system ranks candidates in the database by fit score — no waiting for applications to trickle in.
6. **Recruiters explore candidates.** They browse matches with scores and explanations, or run custom searches.
7. **Candidates are added to the pipeline.** The recruiter adds strong candidates to the job's hiring stages.
8. **The team moves candidates through stages.** Shortlisted → Interview Scheduled → Offer.
9. **Feedback and interview notes are recorded.** Each step is documented.
10. **A decision is made.** The hiring manager advances or rejects with an AI-assisted justification on file.

---

### Workflow 2 — Candidate builds a profile and finds a job

1. **The candidate signs up.** They create an account and fill in basic details.
2. **They upload their resume.** The platform extracts skills, work history, and education automatically.
3. **They review and confirm.** Anything uncertain can be edited before being added to the profile.
4. **They set their preferences.** Salary range, preferred location, notice period, job types.
5. **They browse recommended jobs.** Each listing shows their personalized match score and a breakdown of where they fit and where they don't.
6. **They apply.** A short cover note is optional.
7. **They track their application.** Status updates appear as the recruiter moves them through stages.
8. **They receive or miss out on an offer.** Feedback is visible when the recruiter has shared it.

---

### Workflow 3 — Sourcer proactively builds a candidate pipeline

1. **The sourcer opens candidate search.**
2. **They describe what they need** — either with filters (location, skills, experience) or in natural language ("early-career data engineer in Pune with Python and SQL, open to work").
3. **Results appear ranked by relevance.** Match scores, skill highlights, and availability signals are visible on each card.
4. **The sourcer clicks into a candidate** to see the full profile without leaving the search page.
5. **They add candidates to a job pipeline** directly from the search results.
6. **The hiring manager reviews the pipeline** and progresses shortlisted candidates.

---

### Workflow 4 — Hiring manager reviews a pipeline and decides

1. **The hiring manager opens a job.**
2. **They view the matched candidates tab.** Candidates are ranked. Each has a score, a skills breakdown, and an AI narrative.
3. **They dive into a candidate.** They see career trajectory, seniority alignment, hidden talent signals, and resume quality.
4. **They simulate a what-if.** "If I remove this non-essential skill requirement, how many more qualified candidates appear?"
5. **They ask for a decision justification.** The AI generates a 2–3 sentence rationale for advancing or rejecting.
6. **They advance the right candidates** and decline others — all documented.

## 6. Unique and Differentiating Features

### Explainable Matching
Most hiring platforms show a score. Transpahire shows the reasoning behind the score. Every match comes with a skill-level breakdown, a confidence indicator, and an optional plain-language narrative. Recruiters understand why a candidate ranks where they do — not just that they do.

### Hidden Talent Detection
The platform identifies candidates who don't list a required skill but have closely related experience that transfers. Someone who knows Kubernetes is flagged as a strong candidate for a Docker-heavy role. This expands the effective talent pool without lowering the bar.

### Candidate Potential Scoring
A separate signal from the match score. It estimates how likely a candidate is to grow into the role based on skill adjacency, recent learning activity, and career progression patterns. Teams can choose to hire for trajectory, not just current fit.

### Skill Graph Intelligence
The platform understands skill relationships, not just skill names. It knows that TypeScript is related to JavaScript, that Docker is a prerequisite for Kubernetes, and that React can transfer to Vue.js. This relational understanding powers better matches than keyword-based systems.

### Dual Search Modes in One Interface
Structured search and semantic (natural language) search work side by side in the same tool. Recruiters can switch between them depending on how well they know what they're looking for.

### Recruiter-Controlled Tuning
Recruiters can adjust how much each skill matters for a specific role and immediately re-rank the candidate list. They can also simulate changes to job requirements and see the downstream impact on the talent pool — before making any permanent changes.

### Career and Seniority Intelligence
The platform reads a candidate's work history and classifies their career pattern (consistent growth, pivot, specialist, generalist) and their seniority fit for the role. This prevents over-leveled or under-leveled hires that look good on paper but fail in practice.

### Drop-Off Risk Prediction
The platform flags candidates who are likely to decline or withdraw — for example, those actively interviewing at multiple companies or with long notice periods relative to the company's typical hiring timeline. Recruiters can prioritize accordingly.

### Fairness Monitoring
Built-in analysis checks if matching results show patterns that could indicate systemic bias — such as consistently lower scores for highly experienced candidates. This is surfaced as a report, not a hidden correction.

### Full Audit Trail on Matching
Every scoring decision is logged with the exact weights, skill-level scores, and match verdict at the time of computation. This creates a complete, reviewable history of how candidates were evaluated.

## 7. Inferred and Upcoming Features

The following features are not yet fully live but are strongly suggested by the platform's existing architecture, UI components, and design patterns.

---

### Messaging and In-Platform Communication

**What it could be:** Direct messaging between recruiters and candidates within the platform — without needing to go to email or LinkedIn.

**Why it makes sense:** The data model already includes a messaging entity. The candidate drawer UI has placeholder action buttons for "message." Given the platform connects recruiters and candidates directly, in-platform outreach is a natural next step.

---

### Notifications and Activity Alerts

**What it could be:** Real-time and email notifications for key events — application status updates, new matches for a job, interview reminders, stage movements.

**Why it makes sense:** The platform generates many events (application submitted, stage changed, feedback added, new candidate matched) but has no explicit notification dispatch layer visible yet. A notification system would dramatically improve engagement from both sides.

---

### Subscription and Billing

**What it could be:** Tiered subscription plans for organizations — with limits on active jobs, search volume, or advanced features unlocked at higher tiers.

**Why it makes sense:** The data model includes subscription and billing entities for both organizations and individual users. A Stripe integration is already referenced. This is infrastructure waiting to be activated.

---

### Interview Scheduling and Calendar Integration

**What it could be:** Scheduling interviews directly within the platform, with calendar sync (Google Calendar, Outlook) and automatic reminders for both sides.

**Why it makes sense:** The platform already stores interview records linked to applications. The natural evolution is to schedule interviews from inside the hiring pipeline view rather than coordinating via external tools.

---

### Candidate Verification and Trust Badges

**What it could be:** Verified profiles — checking that listed skills, certifications, or education are legitimate through third-party or self-attestation.

**Why it makes sense:** A verification entity already exists in the data model for both candidates and organizations. Trust signals would increase confidence in candidate profiles and differentiate the platform from unverified databases.

---

### Collaborative Hiring and Scorecards

**What it could be:** Structured interview scorecards that interviewers fill out, with individual ratings that roll up into a team consensus score.

**Why it makes sense:** Interviewers are already a recognized role with access to candidate profiles. The next logical step is giving them a structured way to record evaluations that feed back into the hiring decision.

---

### Candidate Engagement Scoring

**What it could be:** A metric showing how engaged a candidate is with the platform — have they recently updated their profile, responded to messages, actively browsing jobs?

**Why it makes sense:** The responsiveness signal already exists as a component. Expanding it into a full engagement score would help recruiters prioritize outreach and reduce ghosting.

---

### Team Collaboration on Candidate Evaluation

**What it could be:** Shared notes, tagging, and discussion threads on candidate profiles — visible to the hiring team working a specific role.

**Why it makes sense:** Multiple roles (hiring manager, recruiter, interviewer) all interact with the same candidates. Currently they do this through application feedback. A shared note layer would improve coordination without requiring everyone to be in the same room.

---

### Job Recommendations for Candidates Based on Market Trends

**What it could be:** Personalized suggestions telling a candidate "roles like yours are growing in demand" or "your skills are trending in these industries."

**Why it makes sense:** The taxonomy already tracks skill demand and trending scores per skill. Surfacing this to candidates as career guidance would be a high-value, low-effort feature.

---

### Org-Level Talent Bench Planning

**What it could be:** A view showing organizations their pipeline of potential candidates for future roles — not just active jobs.

**Why it makes sense:** The talent pool intelligence and similar candidates features already show pool depth per role. Extending this into forward-looking bench planning would appeal to enterprise hiring teams who think in quarters, not individual reqs.

---

### OAuth Login via LinkedIn and Google

**What it could be:** Sign in with LinkedIn or Google, with optional profile data import from LinkedIn.

**Why it makes sense:** The frontend login UI already has Google and LinkedIn OAuth buttons wired in. These flows are designed and partially implemented. Full activation would reduce signup friction significantly, especially for candidates.

## 8. Product Strengths

**Depth of matching intelligence.**
Most ATS tools move candidates from column to column. Transpahire understands why a candidate is a good fit at the skill level, the career level, and the seniority level — simultaneously. This depth is rare outside enterprise-grade tools that cost an order of magnitude more.

**Candidate-centric experience.**
Transpahire treats candidates as first-class users, not just data entries. They get match scores, explanations, resume feedback, and full application visibility. This is uncommon in ATS products, which typically optimize entirely for the recruiter.

**Semantic understanding of skills.**
Because skills are structured in a relational graph — not a flat list — the platform understands context. This means "5 years of React" counts toward a "Next.js" job requirement. Keyword-based systems miss this entirely.

**Tunable and transparent.**
Recruiters can see why a candidate ranked the way they did and change the inputs. Most AI hiring tools are black boxes. Transpahire shows its work and lets humans override it.

**Built for scale without complexity.**
The system handles large volumes of candidates, skills, and jobs with fast search and indexed recommendations — while keeping the user interface simple and task-focused.

**Fairness and auditability built in.**
Matching decisions are logged, fairness is monitored, and every score is explainable. These are not bolt-on features — they are first-class parts of the product.

**Modular and flexible hiring workflows.**
Each job can have its own pipeline stages, skill weights, and team assignments. This respects that different roles require different processes, without making the platform complex to configure.

## 9. Positioning Notes

### Transpahire vs. Traditional ATS

Traditional ATS tools (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday) are workflow management systems. They track applications, stages, and statuses well — but they do nothing to help you find or evaluate candidates. Transpahire adds a matching and intelligence layer that traditional ATS tools entirely lack.

**Position as:** *"An ATS with a brain."*

---

### Transpahire vs. Job Boards

Job boards (LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, Naukri) aggregate demand and supply but do not evaluate fit. A job post on LinkedIn returns hundreds of applications sorted only by recency or keyword hits. Transpahire ranks and explains fit — it replaces the manual review step with machine intelligence.

**Position as:** *"What happens after the job board — intelligent evaluation, not just aggregation."*

---

### Transpahire vs. AI Screening Tools

AI screening tools (HireVue, Pymetrics) focus on video interviews or behavioral assessment. Transpahire focuses on skills, experience, and role fit. These are complementary — Transpahire works pre-interview, not during it.

**Position as:** *"Pre-interview intelligence — know who to call before you pick up the phone."*

### The Differentiated Story

Most hiring tools are designed for the recruiter's workflow. Transpahire is designed for the hiring outcome.

It does this by treating matching as a first principle — not as an add-on search filter, but as the core product. Every feature (skill taxonomy, career intelligence, tunable weights, fairness monitoring, what-if simulation) exists to make the match between person and role more accurate, more explainable, and more trustworthy.

The result is faster shortlisting, fewer wrong hires, and a candidate experience that respects both sides of the process.

**Target market sweet spot:**
- Tech-forward companies that hire at volume (50–500 hires per year)
- Companies with structured skills requirements (engineering, data, product, design)
- Hiring teams that are frustrated with black-box AI tools or manual keyword filtering
- Companies in markets with competitive talent pools where finding hidden fits matters

**Key messages for landing pages:**
1. "Find candidates who fit, not just candidates who applied."
2. "Scores that explain themselves."
3. "Matching that understands skills — not just keywords."
4. "Built for hiring decisions your team can stand behind."
5. "The missing layer between your job post and your shortlist."
