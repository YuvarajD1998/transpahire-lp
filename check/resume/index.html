Tools / Check a résumé

See your profile as a job reads it.

The same parser that reads a job description reads a résumé, and it scores the document rather than the person. You get the score, the suggestions behind it, and the skills that would open the most additional roles. No account to see it.

Paste it in

PDF, Word or plain text. The parser reads structure, so the version you actually send to employers is the one worth checking.

Not switched on yet. This check runs on the same services the product runs, and putting them behind a public address needs a decision about rate limits and a size cap that has not been made. A form that appeared to submit into nothing would be worse than one that says so. Until it is live, read what the candidate side does — the first ten minutes of it are this, on your own requisition, with a person to argue with.

What it will not tell you

Three things a tool like this usually claims, and will not here:

  • a list of jobs you would get
  • a salary you could command
  • a percentile against other candidates

A percentile would need a population to compare you against, and telling you what you could earn would be a guess dressed as data. The score is about the document — how much of it a job can actually read — which is the part you can change this afternoon.

It scores the document, not you

A quality score of 62 does not mean the person is a 62. It means the file is leaving evidence on the floor — add measurable outcomes and reduce generic language are the two that move it most. That distinction is the whole reason the number is publishable.

What comes back

Four things, and one of them is a list of gaps.

The gaps are the useful part. A score tells you where you are; the gaps tell you what to do on Saturday.

01

A profile quality score, and the specific suggestions behind it.

02

The skills the parser read out of the document, in the tiers a job would read them in.

03

The skills that would open the most additional roles.

04

The skills it could not place in the taxonomy, and what happens to them.

The fourth one is the same escalation the hiring side gets. When the parser meets a skill it cannot place in the taxonomy it does not guess and it does not drop it: it says so, and a person decides — use the suggested mapping, keep as new, discard. On your side that matters because a skill silently discarded is a role you never appear for.

What happens to what you paste. The file is processed, the report comes back to you, and it is not retained unless you create a profile — which this page does not ask you to do. Nothing is added to a candidate database, nothing is shared, and there is no account to create before you see the result. The privacy policy needs a paragraph about this page before it goes live — it is a draft, and a page that accepts a document is a processing activity.

If you do create one, the same score and the same reasoning are what a recruiter sees on their side of the platform. That symmetry is the point of the candidate product: the same score the recruiter sees .

The score the recruiter sees, is the score you see.

Not a candidate-facing summary of a private number. The same figure, the same reasoning, the same evidence.
