Tools / Check a job description

Run a job description through the real thing.

Paste the requisition that has been open for nine weeks. You get the tiered skills, the shape findings, the language flags and the skills the parser could not place — from the services the product runs, not a lighter version of them.

Paste it in

The whole posting, formatting and all. The parser reads structure, so pasting only the bullet list gives it less to work with than the real thing.

Not switched on yet. This check runs on the same services the product runs, and putting them behind a public address needs a decision about rate limits and a size cap that has not been made. A form that appeared to submit into nothing would be worse than one that says so. Until it is live, book the half-hour — the first ten minutes of it are this, on your own requisition, with a person to argue with.

What it will not tell you

Four numbers you would get from something else, and will not get here:

  • a pool size for your role
  • a count of candidates who match it
  • a score distribution
  • a time-to-fill estimate

All four would imply we hold a pool of candidates matched to your role. We do not, for your role, today — and a number invented to look qualified is a commitment made by a marketing page. What you get back is what the services actually computed about the document you pasted.

Twenty seconds, and it is your own text

Nothing on this page is a demo dataset. The tiers are read out of your description, the flagged words are your words, and the skills it could not place are yours — which is also why it is more useful than any screenshot we could show you.

What comes back

Four things, all of them shipped.

Not a preview of a roadmap. Every one of these runs inside the product today, against every requisition that goes through it.

01

Your skills, in the four tiers

Every skill the parser reads out of the description, sorted into critical, required, preferred and bonus — the tiers the engine actually scores against, in the product's own tones. This is the part most people have never seen: a job description is a weighting, and yours already is one whether it was written that way or not.

02

What the shape of the requisition costs you

The optimizer's findings. A critical skill is a hard gate applied before scoring, so each one is a pool you never see — and the panel names it rather than leaving you to wonder why the list is short.

03

The language that is narrowing the pool

The structural fairness check reads the requirements themselves. It uses and holds no demographic data. It will tell you that the word rockstar is in your description, and what that word does to who applies.

04

The skills it could not place

When the parser meets a skill it cannot map to the taxonomy it does not guess and it does not drop it. You get the list, the mapping it suspects, and the three choices a person makes in the product: use the suggested mapping, keep it as new, or discard it.

Structural fairness

Needs review

How this job description narrows the candidate pool. It looks at the requirements themselves — no demographic data is used or held.

  • ⚑ “rockstar” exclusionary jargon
  • ⚑ “recent graduate” overspecifying graduation recency may limit experienced returners
  • ⚑ 7 critical skills almost nobody clears all seven
○

Not enough scored candidates to rate distribution yet. We need 50; this role has 47.

Finding

Too many criticals

Every critical skill is a hard gate applied before scoring, so each one you add is a pool you cannot see. A requisition with seven of them is not a high bar, it is an empty list.

Finding

A near-impossible combination

The requirements are individually reasonable and jointly rare. Named, so it is a decision rather than a mystery.

Finding

No soft-skill tier

Nothing in the description maps to a soft skill, which means the whole evaluation runs on technical coverage alone.

What happens to what you paste. The document is processed and the report is returned to you. It is not retained beyond that unless you ask us to keep it, and there is no box pre-ticked to make you. Nothing is added to a candidate database, nothing is shared, and there is no account to create before you see the result. The privacy policy needs a paragraph about this page before it goes live — it is a draft, and a page that accepts a document is a processing activity.
