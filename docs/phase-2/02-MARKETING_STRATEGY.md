Category, audience, problem, differentiation, and the one idea the site is built
to deliver.

Reconciled against `[OVERVIEW]`. Where `[OVERVIEW]` § 9 states a position, that

## 1. First principle — the transformation

**Before.** A recruiter runs a keyword search, gets a list they cannot rank,
reads forty profiles to find six worth a call, forms a private opinion about
each, and defends that opinion in a hiring meeting from memory. Candidates hear
nothing. Three weeks later nobody can reconstruct why anyone was passed over.

**After.** The list arrives ranked and scored, and every position carries its
reasons — five scored dimensions, which required skills are covered, which are
partial, which are missing, a plain-language narrative, and signals the recruiter
would never have derived by hand: is this person over-levelled, are they likely
to grow into the role, are they likely to reply, are they likely to withdraw. The
recruiter's judgement is applied *to an argument* rather than assembled from raw
material. They can change what matters and watch the ranking change. The hiring
manager reads the same argument. The candidate sees the same score. The record —
including the exact weights used — survives the meeting.

**In one line:** *hiring stops being a private opinion and becomes a shared,
inspectable, adjustable argument.*

`[OVERVIEW]` § 9 says the same thing from the other end: *"Most hiring tools are
designed for the recruiter's workflow. Transpahire is designed for the hiring
outcome."*

## 2. Category

`[OVERVIEW]` opens with its own answer, and it is the right one:

> **Transpahire is an intelligent hiring platform built for modern talent teams.**
> … It sits between a traditional applicant tracking system and a smart talent
> marketplace. It is lighter and faster than enterprise ATS tools, but far more
> intelligent than a basic job board.

**Recommendation: use `[OVERVIEW]`'s own phrase — "intelligent hiring platform".**

It survives every test the alternatives fail: it is what the product is, it is
not a crowded phrase, it does not concede the ATS frame, it does not orphan the
candidate side, and it reconciles with the brand system's AUTHORITATIVE tagline
*"Hiring intelligence for teams that care about quality"* `[BRAND]` without an
edit. The word doing the work is **intelligent**; the word doing the positioning
is **platform** — it is where the hire happens, not a layer on someone else's
system.

Rejected, with reasons: *talent intelligence platform* (means workforce/skills-
inventory analytics — a different category); *intelligent sourcing platform*
(sourcing is one module of fourteen); *AI talent marketplace* (tells a recruiter
they will be sold candidates); *recruiting intelligence layer* ("layer" implies
integration with an incumbent, and `[OVERVIEW]` § 7 puts every integration in
upcoming); *AI-powered hiring platform* ("AI-powered" is the weakest word
available and `[OVERVIEW]` never uses it).

Buyers compare against three things, and `[OVERVIEW]` § 9 addresses each
directly: the incumbent **ATS**, **job boards**, and **AI screening tools**. See
§ 8.

## 3. "An ATS with a brain"

`[OVERVIEW]` § 9 sanctions this line explicitly, scoped to the ATS comparison:

> Traditional ATS tools are workflow management systems … Transpahire adds a
> matching and intelligence layer that traditional ATS tools entirely lack.
> **Position as: "An ATS with a brain."**

**Adopt it, in the scope the source gives it.** It is authoritative, it is
memorable, and against an incumbent ATS it lands immediately.

**Recommendation on scope, offered as analysis and not as an override:** use it
where `[OVERVIEW]` uses it — on the ATS comparison, in sales conversations, and
on `/product/matching`'s comparison section. **Do not make it the hero or the
category statement**, for three reasons worth knowing before deciding:

1. It concedes the frame. A buyer who reads "ATS" evaluates on integrations, SSO,
   compliance reporting and configurability — the axes where `[OVERVIEW]` § 1
   deliberately positions Transpahire as *lighter*, and `01 § 8` records as
   non-capabilities.
2. It has no room for the candidate side, which is half the product and a stated
   strength `[OVERVIEW]` § 8.
3. "With a brain" makes intelligence an attachment to a system of record. In this
   product the explanation *is* the output.

Note that `[OVERVIEW]` itself does not use it as the primary framing — its own
first sentence is "intelligent hiring platform". The recommendation is simply to
keep the source's own hierarchy: **category in the hero, ATS-with-a-brain in the
comparison.** If the owner wants it in the hero, it is their call and it is
defensible; the site works either way.

## 4. Audience

### Primary: recruiters and sourcers

`[OVERVIEW]` § 2 groups them under one goal-set — find the best candidates
quickly, search by filters *and* by description, build pipelines efficiently.
The homepage speaks to that person.

Why them rather than the hiring manager or the head of talent: they are the
primary users, the pain the product removes is theirs by the hour, and every
Tier-1 capability is aimed at their daily loop.

### Secondary: hiring managers and job managers

`[OVERVIEW]` § 2 groups these too — define what a great hire looks like, review
efficiently, decide with support. They must understand the value in under a
minute without a page of their own at launch. Their story is decision quality:
score breakdown, narrative, career trajectory, seniority alignment, potential,
what-if, and AI-generated decision justification. Served by section 06, section
08 and `/product/matching`.

### Target market — now sourced

`[OVERVIEW]` § 9, verbatim: tech-forward companies hiring at volume (**50–500
hires per year**), with structured skills requirements (**engineering, data,
product, design**), teams frustrated with black-box AI tools or manual keyword
filtering, and **competitive talent markets where finding hidden fits matters**.

This closes an open item: the current homepage's "50–500 roles per year" is
**sourced** and may stay — corrected to *hires*, which is what `[OVERVIEW]` says.
The last clause is the most useful sentence in `[OVERVIEW]` § 9 for copy purposes,
because it names the condition under which hidden-talent detection stops being a
feature and becomes the reason to buy.

### Second audience: the candidate

`[OVERVIEW]` § 8 makes candidate-centricity a **stated product strength**:
*"Transpahire treats candidates as first-class users, not just data entries …
This is uncommon in ATS products, which typically optimize entirely for the
recruiter."*

A homepage that addresses recruiters and candidates equally addresses neither.
The split:

| Treatment | Decision |
|---|---|
| Homepage section | **One** — section 10, framed as *evidence for the transparency claim*, addressed to the recruiter |
| Dedicated page | **`/for-candidates`. MUST HAVE.** Carries the candidate narrative in full |
| Separate narrative | **Yes, on that page only.** Profile → résumé intelligence → matched roles with scores → why you match → gaps → apply → track |
| Secondary CTA | **Yes** — a quiet "Looking for a role?" in the nav utility slot and footer. Never in a hero, never a button |

## 7. Differentiation — the four that carry

`[OVERVIEW]` § 6 lists ten. Ten is not a message. These four carry the site;
the rest are evidence for them.

**1. Explainable matching.** Score, five dimensions, per-skill covered/partial/
missing, a plain-language narrative, and an audit record holding the exact
weights and skill-level scores at the time of computation `[OVERVIEW]` § 3, § 6.
Competitors show a score and a keyword list.

**2. Skill graph intelligence.** The platform knows TypeScript relates to
JavaScript, that Docker is a prerequisite for Kubernetes, that React transfers to
Vue `[OVERVIEW]` § 6. Hidden-talent detection, semantic similarity, career
progression and clusters all fall out of one structured substrate. **The moat.**

**3. Recruiter-controlled tuning.** Adjust a skill's importance and re-rank
instantly; simulate a requirement change and see the pool move before committing.
`[OVERVIEW]` § 8: *"Transpahire shows its work and lets humans override it."*
Rare, demonstrable, and the answer to the black-box objection.

**4. Candidates are first-class.** Same score, same breakdown, real status, AI
résumé feedback — a stated product strength and *"uncommon in ATS products"*
`[OVERVIEW]` § 8.

Demoted to supporting evidence rather than headline claims: potential score,
career trajectory, seniority alignment, drop-off risk, responsiveness (a strong
cluster — market them together as *signals you could not have derived by hand*,
on `/product/matching`); dual search modes (excellent, but a *how* not a *why*);
talent pool intelligence and the skill heatmap (distinctive, low urgency);
fairness monitoring and the audit trail (**confirmed features, gated claims** —
see § 9).

## 9. Trust as a design theme

The progression *AI recommends → AI explains → human reviews → human controls →
decision is traceable* is now **mechanically true at every step**: matching
recommends, the explanation and narrative explain, the recruiter reviews, weights
and simulation give control, and the audit trail records the weights and
skill-level scores at computation time `[OVERVIEW]` § 3, § 6.

**The features exist. Permission to market them does not follow.** Automated
hiring tools are regulated in Transpahire's likely markets — NYC LL144, the EU AI
Act's high-risk classification for employment systems, EEOC guidance — and
`docs/content-integrity.md` § 4 requires named legal sign-off. That gate is about
liability, not about whether the code is written.

The distinction to hold in every draft:

| Publishable after sign-off | Not publishable |
|---|---|
| "Every scoring decision is logged with the weights used" | "Fully auditable" |
| "Fairness patterns are surfaced as a report, not silently corrected" | "Bias-free", "fair by design" |
| "You can see and explain the reasoning behind a decision" | "Defensible", "compliant" |

Describe the mechanism; never claim the outcome. `[OVERVIEW]` § 8's own phrasing
— *"surfaced as a report, not a hidden correction"* — is a good model: it is a
statement about behaviour, not a promise about consequences.

Design carries what copy cannot. Section 11 is typographic and quiet — a
progression set in type on ink, not a grid of shield icons. A page that *looks*
auditable makes the claim before a word is read `DESIGN.md § 1`.

## 11. The 30-second answer

> **If a recruiter spends only 30 seconds on the homepage, what one thing should
> they understand that they would not get from an ordinary ATS or AI recruiting
> platform?**

**That every ranking comes with its reasons — and that the reasons are built on
what skills actually mean, not on what words appeared in a résumé.**

Not "we use AI to match candidates". That each position on the list is an
argument they can inspect, disagree with, and re-tune — produced by a system that
knows Docker leads to Kubernetes and will tell them when a candidate is
over-levelled, unlikely to reply, or likely to withdraw.

> **Why should they believe it?**

Because the site shows the panel rather than describing it: a score out of 100,
five named dimensions, five of six required skills covered, one **partial** with
the adjacent skill that transfers named, one missing, a two-sentence narrative,
and a seniority flag. `[OVERVIEW]` § 6 is the warrant — *"Most hiring platforms
show a score. Transpahire shows the reasoning behind the score."* Its specificity
is the proof; nobody fabricates a *partial* skill state.

> **What product visualisation proves it?**

The **explanation panel** — section 06, the centre of the page. A candidate is
selected; the score resolves; five dimensions fill; covered, partial and missing
skills separate; the narrative writes in; the seniority and risk signals arrive
last. Spec: `08 § 3`. Its strongest single detail is the **partial** state, because
it is where the skill graph becomes visible in the UI.

> **What interaction makes it memorable?**

Two, and the page can afford both now that neither is gated:

1. **Selecting a different candidate and watching the whole argument change**
   (§ 06) — two clicks and the reasoning is understood as real and per-candidate.
2. **Dragging a skill's importance and watching the list reorder** (§ 08) — the
   visitor changes the outcome. `[OVERVIEW]` § 3's tuning is confirmed, so this is
   buildable, and it is the single most differentiating thing a visitor can *do*.

> **What CTA should they take?**

**"Bring a role you're struggling to fill"** → a demo. Not a free trial: the
value shows against a real requisition and a real pool, and `[OVERVIEW]` § 7 puts
self-serve billing in upcoming, so there is nothing to self-serve into. See
`09 § 6`.
